# Script writing: the retention loop

Applied in step 1 of every run. It is the structure of the script, not an optional style. Language and voice come from `style-preferences.md`.

## The idea
Dopamine is the prediction chemical: it fires while the viewer waits to learn what happens. A vending machine (same input, same output) is flat; a slot machine (unknown outcome) holds attention. A script keeps people watching by always having one unresolved prediction open.

Every stretch runs a four-step loop: **Stakes → Big question → Head fake → Rehook**, and the rehook opens the next loop. A 60 s video has one main loop with smaller loops nested inside it.

## The four steps

### 1. Stakes (beats 1–2, about 8 s)
Without a reason to care there are no predictions. Three parts: **a character** the viewer can picture (a person, a group, "you"), **something at risk**, **urgency** (a date, a clock, "this week"). Stakes means personally relevant, not life or death. For news, the stake is what the event changes for the viewer. Before beat 3 the viewer must know who, what is at risk and why now.

### 2. Big question (beats 2–4)
Give just enough specific information to make a prediction. "She told me something that changed my strategy for six months" works; "you won't believe what happened" is an empty teaser. Add context before an important reveal so the viewer can guess first. Every script needs one big question near the start that runs to the end.

### 3. Head fake (the middle, at least two when the source has them)
A reveal that breaks the viewer's prediction. Work out what a normal viewer predicts, then deliver an answer that contradicts it but is clearly logical on the spot. Plant the clue earlier. **Never invent a twist**: it must come from the real facts of the source (a number, a cause, a reversal). No reversal in the source: say so in the script table and let the big question and rehooks carry the loop.

### 4. Rehook (every join between beats)
Close one loop and open the next in the same sentence: "but…", "and the surprise?", "and the reason?", "what happened next?", "why though?". Write these in the video's language and vary them; never the same one twice in a row. Do not end a beat on a full stop with nothing pending.

Vertical feeds are a swipe away: the hook and its first caption words land within 1.5 s, with the stake or question inside the first five words.

## Mapping to a 60 s script
| Beats | Loop role | The script must |
|---|---|---|
| 01–02 | Stakes (+ question in the hook) | Say who, what is at risk or changes, why now |
| 02–04 | Big question | Load one specific question by about 12 s |
| 05–13 | Head fakes + rehooks | Use real reversals from the source; end every beat by opening the next |
| 14 | Payoff + final rehook | Answer the big question, then open one last question; no plain summary |
| 15 | End card | Follow CTA and the comment question as the last rehook |

## By script type
| Type | Adjust |
|---|---|
| Explainer | Head fakes come from the mechanism ("it isn't the price, it's the delay") |
| News breakdown | Stakes first ("what this changes for you"); receipts are the proof beats |
| Story / case study | One character; chronology is the loop; the head fake is the turn in the story |
| Myth-buster | State the belief in beat 1, plant why it feels true, reverse it at the midpoint |
| Tutorial / product / opinion (`LOOP=light`) | Stakes, one question, one reveal, a closing question |

## Script table: required columns
`time | line (chunks) | mode + rule # | visual | loop tag | section`. Loop tags: `stakes`, `question`, `fake`, `rehook`, `payoff`. Under the table:
1. **Big question:** the question and the beat that loads it.
2. **Head fakes:** beat numbers, what the viewer expects vs what the source says.
3. **Final rehook:** the question the last beat leaves open.

## Self-check before showing the script
- [ ] Stakes (character, risk, urgency) clear by beat 2
- [ ] A specific big question loaded by about 12 s
- [ ] Head fakes grounded in the source and set up earlier (or "none" with the reason)
- [ ] Every join reopens a question
- [ ] The last beat leaves something open
- [ ] No vague teasers, no invented facts or numbers; disputed figures flagged or dropped
- [ ] Concrete over vague: a name, number, date, place. Ask the question out loud, like a friend.
- [ ] If it reads like the flat version, rewrite it.
